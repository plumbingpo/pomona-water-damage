Hardwood Floor Water Damage Help in Pomona

Wood floors react to water quickly. Boards take on moisture from below and from the edges, swell against each other and start to cup, with the edges rising higher than the center. Cupping alone is no reason to give up on the floor. When drying starts within the first few days, solid hardwood frequently lies flat again. The key is pulling moisture out of the boards and the subfloor underneath at a steady, controlled pace.

Drying mats are how our team handles wet wood floors. A vacuum unit attached to the sealed mats lifts water out by way of the seams and the wood itself, and dehumidifiers hold the room air at a dry level. Compared with carpet or drywall it is slower, commonly a week or more. Sanding has to wait until the wood is fully dry. A cupped floor sanded early ends up crowned afterward.

Warning Signs

  • Swollen trim along the edge of the floor near the leak
  • Planks pulling loose from the subfloor or peaking where they meet
  • Boards shrinking apart and leaving gaps following a rushed dry out
  • A hazy, whitened or flaking finish where the water sat
  • A springy or soft spot in the floor that was not there earlier
  • A washboard feel underfoot from edges that stand taller than board centers
Behind the Surface

What Hides Behind It

Rusting Fasteners

Staples and flooring nails corrode when moisture lingers, and their grip weakens. Dark marks from that rust soak into the boards and can be too deep to sand out.

Drying Too Fast

High heat or too much airflow pulls moisture from the surface faster than the core can follow. Lasting gaps and split or cracked boards are the usual outcome.

Sanding Too Early

Sanding cupped boards flat while they are still wet removes the raised edges. Once drying is complete, the middles sit higher and you have a crowned floor.

Wet Subfloor

The subfloor can hold moisture long after the boards feel dry. Leave it wet and the cupping returns or mold grows up from below.

The Clock

The Damage Timeline

The first few hours

Seepage Below the Boards

Most of the water ends up beneath the boards, having run down through the joints. Underneath, the wood remains wet even after the face has been dried.

Hours 24 to 48

Cupping Starts

Swelling sets in and board edges lift. You might notice haze in the topcoat. Growth is able to begin below, on raw wood and on the subfloor panels.

Day 3 to 7

Pressure Builds Up

Swelling builds pressure across the floor. Boards crush against each other, fasteners loosen and sections can begin to lift off the subfloor.

After a week

Permanent Damage

Black stains, buckled boards and mold beneath the flooring are now probable. By now replacement, not drying, is the answer for most of the affected section.

What We Do

What Happens After You Call

1

Take Baseline Readings

Readings are taken in the boards and the subfloor, and on dry flooring close by as a reference. The comparison shows how far down moisture reached and how likely the floor is to recover.

2

Extract Standing Water

We extract any standing water immediately and clear furniture and rugs from the wet section. Cutting off the water entering the joints early keeps swelling down.

3

Install the Mat System

A vacuum system hooked to mats sealed on the surface lifts moisture out of the boards from above. Dehumidifiers control the room so the wood dries evenly without cracking.

4

Track Progress and Report

Readings are logged every one to two days and the equipment stays until the wet zone equals the dry comparison areas. At that point you hear from us if it needs nothing, some replacement boards, or a refinish.
